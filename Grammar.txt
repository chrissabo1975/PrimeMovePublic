The Prime Move: A Universal Grammar of Change
Author: Chris Sabo (C.m. Nagy) Affiliations: Independent Researcher, Needlework Tattoo Co. Date: April 2026 Status: Position Paper / Framework Introduction
Abstract
I propose that a single five-stage generative cycle — Split → Tension → Failed Merge → Scar → Decay — underlies every process that produces persistent structure. This “Prime Move” is not a model of any specific domain. It is a universal grammar of change itself.
Derived from four minimal axioms about distinction and irreversibility, the grammar predicts that scar accumulation follows the Fibonacci recurrence, growth ratios converge to the golden ratio φ ≈ 1.618, and hierarchical complexity unfolds through a fixed sequence of six tiers reachable by three distinct pathways (Spiral, Optimization, Aggregation).
It works as a translation layer: the same five-stage shape appears in mitosis, conversation, tattooing, and language evolution. This paper introduces the grammar, demonstrates it in action, and argues that it meets every criterion for a true universal grammar: generativity, recursivity, substrate independence, and falsifiability.
1. The Problem of Disciplines
One simple sentence — “The dog is chasing the bouncing ball downhill” — needs physics for gravity and momentum, biology for muscle and instinct, psychology for desire and attention, and linguistics for syntax and meaning.
Each discipline explains its piece brilliantly. None explains the whole.
This is not a failure of the fields. It is the natural result of how knowledge is built: powerful theories earn precision by staying inside clear boundaries. But the cost is fragmentation. We have no shared language for describing change itself — the process by which any system, in any domain, moves from one state to the next and leaves something permanent behind.
The Prime Move is that shared language.
2. The Grammar: Five Stages, Three Axioms
The Prime Move is a five-stage cycle that every process generating persistent structure must follow:
Stage	Symbol	Function
Split	S	A distinction appears where there was unity
Tension	T	Imbalance or pressure forms
Failed Merge	FM	Attempted resolution that leaves residue
Scar	Sc	Residue stabilizes as persistent structure
Decay	D	Release that enables the next cycle
This cycle is not arbitrary. It is the smallest structure that can be derived from four axioms:
    0.   For any structure to exist, an original undifferentiated state must be violated by the act of distinction. This violation is irreversible.

	1.	Unity precedes distinction (Axiom 1)
	2.	Distinction is necessary for any non-trivial process (Axiom 2)
	3.	Distinction cannot be perfectly erased — it always leaves a scar (Axiom 3)
From these axioms the five-stage cycle follows necessarily. With one additional assumption (a one-generation maturation delay before a scar can produce new distinctions), the math emerges cleanly: scar counts follow the Fibonacci recurrence, ratios converge to φ ≈ 1.618, and the system becomes scale-invariant and self-perpetuating. No free parameters. No hidden constants.
3. Why “Universal Grammar”?
I borrow the term from linguistics, where universal grammar means the innate structural rules underlying all human languages. Here it means a set of structural rules that generate all processes producing persistent, hierarchical complexity.
A true universal grammar must satisfy four tests:
	•	Generativity — the five stages combine recursively to produce unbounded complexity
	•	Recursivity — the output of one cycle (a scar) becomes the input to the next
	•	Substrate independence — the same cycle works in skin, cells, words, or ideas
	•	Falsifiability — specific predictions can be tested and can fail
The Prime Move passes all four.
4. Demonstration: One Grammar, Many Domains
Watch what happens inside a single tattoo session at Needlework Tattoo Co.
	•	Split — The needle touches clean skin. A new distinction appears: marked vs. unmarked.
	•	Tension — The client’s skin reacts; the artist feels the exact pressure needed. Imbalance forms between intention and execution.
	•	Failed Merge — The line is drawn. It can never be perfectly undone. Ink is now under the skin forever.
	•	Scar — The line stabilizes as permanent structure — part of the final design, part of the client’s body, part of the artist’s muscle memory.
	•	Decay — The artist lifts the machine, breathes, and the system resets for the next line.
Now run the exact same five-stage cycle in three other domains:
Domain	Split	Tension	Failed Merge	Scar	Decay
Cell mitosis	Chromosomes separate	Spindle fibers pull	Incomplete division	Two daughter cells	Cell cycle repeats
Conversation	Speaker/listener distinction	Meaning gap	Imperfect understanding	Shared understanding	Conversation ends
Language evolution	New word emerges	Communication pressure	Incomplete adoption	Stable lexical item	Word becomes archaic
The substrate changes. The shape never does.
That identical five-stage engine is what a universal grammar looks like.
5. The Ladder and the Pathways
Recursive application of the Prime Move produces a fixed sequence of six complexity tiers:
	1.	Linear (1D) — simple sequences, no branching
	2.	Hierarchical (2D) — nested parent-child structures
	3.	Fractal (fractional D) — self-similar scaling
	4.	Wave (3D oscillation) — feedback cycles
	5.	Dendritic (3D networks) — redundant connectivity
	6.	Perpetual (4D time-binding) — self-sustaining recursion
At Tier 3 (Fractal) the grammar branches into three distinct generative pathways, each with its own signature:
	•	Spiral — asymmetric tension + directional memory (golden angle, φ ratios)
	•	Optimization — symmetric constraints + efficiency pressure (Murray’s law, 120° angles)
	•	Aggregation — stochastic diffusion + local rules (DLA fractal dimension ≈ 1.7)
All three pathways can lead to the higher tiers. The grammar is branching, not linear.
6. The Falsifiable Core
A universal grammar that cannot fail is not science. The Prime Move makes sharp, risky predictions:
	•	Spiral systems will show φ-family convergence within measurement error.
	•	Optimization systems will show Murray exponents near 3 or 120° angles.
	•	Aggregation systems will show DLA fractal dimensions near 1.7 in 2D.
	•	Mixed-pathway systems will show higher fractal dimension than pure Aggregation.
	•	No system will skip tiers.
These have already been checked across fifteen systems (Paper 4.5). The most distinctive untested prediction: during a tattoo session, artists in flow will underestimate elapsed time while clients in pain will overestimate it — a direct consequence of time being scar-accumulation rate.
7. The Gift: A Translation Layer
The Prime Move is not here to replace domain-specific theories. It is here to translate between them.
A linguist studying Merge can map it to Split. A biologist studying Murray’s law can map it to Optimization. A physicist studying entropy can map it to scar accumulation. A psychologist studying flow can map it to Perpetual-tier operation.
It gives researchers a shared reference when they notice the same shape appearing in their own work.
8. Conclusion
The Prime Move is a minimal, axiomatic, empirically testable universal grammar of change. It is not a theory of everything. It is a theory of structure that persists — and a demonstration that such structure always follows the same five-stage cycle, whether the medium is skin, cells, conversation, or ideas.
